Pipeline for Identification of Composite Transposons from Assembly graphs

The element is gone from the contigs.
Its shape is still in the graph.

A composite transposon is cargo — resistance genes, a degradation pathway — flanked by two copies of the same insertion sequence. Short-read assemblers collapse those copies, so the element rarely survives as a contig. What survives is a cycle in the assembly graph. PICOTA finds those cycles and scores them.

01 · The idea

How a composite transposon becomes a cycle

Figure 1. A composite transposon in the genome and in the assembly graph. insertion sequence (IS), cargo, host chromosome. Reads from the two IS copies are indistinguishable, so the assembler keeps one IS node, at twice the read depth, and IS + cargo close a cycle.

02 · Why not just read the contigs?

The data is there. No contig carries it whole.

An element counts as recovered only when a single contig — or a single detected cycle — carries at least 95% of it in one alignment. Across 520 simulated elements:

With two genomic IS copies and nothing repeating inside the element, SPAdes contigs recover 52–78%. One more copy of that IS takes them to 5–7.5%. The graph holds 95–100% in each case.

How does the search work?

Each contig is two nodes, one per strand. A depth-first search walks them; an edge back to a node still on the stack — or to its reverse complement — closes a cycle. The replay is the actual event order of PICOTA's dfs_iterative, and the cycles and scores are PICOTA's actual output on the graph.

insertion sequence xenobiotic resistance other ORFs

Figure 3. Cycle detection on an assembly graph. (a) The assembly graph — the hand-drawn demo or the bundled P. nitroreducens graph — one node per contig, sized by length; the DFS replay colours nodes on the stack and the back-edges that close cycles, and the selected cycle is drawn as its walk. (b) Circular map of the selected cycle: contigs in walk order on the outer ring, PICOTA's IS and cargo hits on the middle ring, Prodigal ORFs (+ strand outside, − strand inside) on the inner ring.

From back-edges to candidates: for each back-edge, all simple paths from its target back to its source (up to path_limit = 25 nodes) are enumerated, sequences are rebuilt from contig overlaps, cycles outside 1–40 kb are dropped, and near-duplicates are removed — first by strand-agnostic contig sets, then by k-mer Jaccard similarity.

    05 · Scoring

    Most cycles are the host's own repeats. The score separates them.

    Detection casts wide on purpose — about 23 candidate cycles per genome to find 4 elements. score3 is bounded 0–100: two gates that can zero it outright, and two equally weighted terms.

    score3 = 100 × IS_gate × length_gate × ( 0.50 · component_fit + 0.50 · cargo_quality )

    identity × coverage of the best IS hit. Gate = 0.5 + 0.5 × quality, or 0 with no IS.

    Shorter than the CT mean (5,850 bp) pays nothing; the gate falls with z-score, to zero at z = 20.

    1 / (1 + |n − 2| / 12). A host-repeat filter: cycles wandering host repeats sit near 11.

    Best CARD / xenobiotic hit, (aln / subject length) × identity. With no hit, a floor of 0.30 keeps novel cargo reportable.

    0/ 100

    The 0.50 / 0.50 split is measured, not chosen: across the 1,272 benchmark candidates it gives the best F1, and its own optimal threshold lands on 50 — the value threshold_final_score ships with.

    Figure 5. score3 evaluated as you move the inputs, with the shipped constants: composite transposon length 5,850 ± 2,586 bp, maximum z of 20, novel-cargo floor 0.30, reporting threshold 50.

    06 · Benchmark

    Six architectures, scored at a threshold of 50.

    Composite transposons implanted into E. coli K-12 MG1655, so the answer is known by construction: ten genomes per architecture, four elements each. An element counts as recovered when one reported cycle covers ≥ 95% of it at ≥ 95% identity. Detection runs at about 17% precision; scoring takes that to 91.2% while keeping 98.5% of the elements.

    Figure 6a. Six simulated architectures, ten genomes and 40 elements each, scored at score3 > 50. Recovered: one reported cycle covers ≥ 95% of the element at ≥ 95% identity. False positives are reported cycles covering no implanted element.

    Known limit. When the flanking IS also occurs inside the cargo (cargo_is_same), only 2 of 40 elements are covered by any candidate cycle. The loss is in detection, not scoring, so no threshold recovers it.

    Add IS copies and the contigs lose the element. The graph does not.

    The 40 baseline elements re-implanted at six flanking-IS copy numbers. MobileElementFinder (MEF) recovers them from a complete chromosome, but from contigs it can only find what a single contig still carries whole.

    Nothing implanted, nothing reported.

      08 · Get started

      Run it on the bundled graph in one command.

      No download, no assembly: detection, scoring and annotation over testNitro.gfa with the shipped settings. Linux, Python ≥ 3.8, BLAST+ and Prodigal.

      Reference databases (CARD, ISfinder/TnCentral, KEGG xenobiotics, known composite transposons) are not in the git repository; the prepared set is available from the authors on request.
